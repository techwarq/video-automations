This tiny machine is one of the craziest rocket engines ever built. It's SpaceX's Raptor — the engine powering Starship. But here's what makes it different. Most rocket engines throw some propellant away to power their turbines. Raptor doesn't. It uses full-flow staged combustion. Two preburners partially burn methane and oxygen separately, creating two hot gas streams that spin two turbines. And here's the crazy part: virtually all the fuel and oxygen eventually go into the main combustion chamber. That gives Raptor incredible efficiency and high chamber pressure. SpaceX also chose methane instead of kerosene — cleaner combustion, better suited for reusability, and potentially useful for producing fuel on Mars. So Raptor isn't just a bigger rocket engine. SpaceX redesigned the entire engine cycle around reusability and efficiency.
